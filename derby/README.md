# Auto Destruction

A car combat game built on Monster Mayhem's town and its New York. You build a gun car in the garage, pick one of three modes (a race, zombie survival or an arena free-for-all), wreck rival cars for scrap, then spend the scrap on better parts. The buildings, the destruction (blasts, glass, fire, collapsing storeys, rubble), the gun effects and the sound mixer are Monster Mayhem's own code, imported as it is; the derby only makes its buildings sturdier, from its own side (`BUILDINGS` in `tuning.js`, `sturdy.js`). Nothing in `src/`, `units/` or `civilian/` was edited for this.

## Play
`npm install` at the repo root, then `node derby/build.mjs`. Open `derby/dist/auto-destruction.html` in Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. It is one self-contained file.

- WASD or the arrow keys: drive. Space: handbrake. Hold it while turning at speed to drift: the back steps out and the car holds the slide on throttle and steering, keeping most of its speed, with smoke and skid marks; a long drift pays a short burst of speed. With the wheel straight it just stops the car. Racing slicks drift least, off-road and monster tyres most (`DRIFT` in `tuning.js`, `drift.js`).
- Mouse: aim the turret (click the view to capture the mouse, Esc to let it go). The aim pulls onto a rival near the crosshair, and the crosshair turns red when it has one. Wheel: camera distance.
- Left click (or J): every gun (main gun, roof guns, door guns, arc coil). Right click (or F, K): every heavy weapon (heavy weapon slot, roof rockets, door rockets).
- Shift: the gadget (nitro, repair kit, shield, oil slick or jump jets), if one is fitted.
- G: the garage, from the start screen and the results (and between waves on the hidden town map).
- R: in the race, back to the last checkpoint if you are lost or going the wrong way (or tap the hint).
- Drive square into a wall at speed (40 or more) to smash through it; glancing hits slide along it. Lamps, bins and rubble go down at any speed. Buildings take a real beating before a storey gives way: rockets and wrecks dent walls rather than delete them, and fire spreads slowly.
- M: sound on or off.
- Phones and tablets (or `?touch=1`): a drive stick on the left (push the way you want to go), and Fire, Rockets, Drift and Nitro buttons on the right. The guns aim themselves at the nearest rival in view (the nearest zombie on the base). Every map and mode works held sideways or upright: the cards shrink to fit short screens, the armour bar sits between the stick and the buttons (across the top when upright), upright phones get a wider view, and Start goes full screen where the browser allows it. The picture quality adjusts itself to the frame rate (`quality.js`; phones start a few steps down; `?q=0` to `?q=6` holds a step).

## Modes and maps
The start card picks one of three modes, each on its own map. To open one directly, add `?map=race`, `base` or `arena` to the page's address. Switching reloads the page. A first visit opens on the arena.

- **Race: Manhattan Circuit** (`race`): a street race through Monster Mayhem's New York, raced over 4 laps by 6 cars with the guns on. A lap is about 2,250 voxels (on Manhattan's grid that is the longest loop the rivers, the park and the bay allow) and the road is about 87 wide (63 at its narrowest), with one row of lots left unbuilt along each run. It runs from the top of the park onto the East River drive, past the king tower and the silver spire, round a long U-turn by the Times Square tower, a tighter U-turn on the river, west through downtown, a sweep at the Battery and north beside the park: mostly fast sweeps, one tight turn. Concrete barriers with made-up sponsor boards and temporary stands shut the cross streets and bridge mouths. Checkpoint gates must be passed in order, so cutting gains nothing. A wrecked car (yours too) comes back at its last gate after 2.5 s, so wrecking someone costs them time. Scrap by place (300, 200, 120, 80, 50, 30 on Normal) on top of 100 for each rival you wreck. A race takes about 2 minutes.
  - Difficulty (Easy, Normal, Hard, under the maps on the start card, kept in the save): the rivals' cars are matched to yours, as the big racing games fill a race with cars of your class, so a better car changes how you win rather than whether there is a race. Each rival gets a body near yours in speed, with the engine and tyres nearest the speed it should have, then its top speed and pull tuned to your car's times the level's share (Easy 0.92, Normal 1.0, Hard 1.05, the front of the grid a little quicker than the back) and your turning. The level also sets how well they drive (how much of their top speed they hold, how near the limit they corner, how late they brake, how steady their aim), the catch-up (Easy and Normal: a rival far ahead eases off and one far behind finds a little more; Hard: none) and the scrap (0.75x, 1x, 1.5x). Every car, yours too, gets a tow tucked in behind another (slipstream: up to 6% more top speed; the HUD says Tow!). The knobs are `RACE` in `tuning.js`. Tested with your car on autopilot in a Viper with slicks and the V8: a strong driver won Easy and Normal by 5 s and 1.5 s and was last on Hard; an average one came 2nd, 5th and 3rd.
- **Zombie Survival: Camp Deadfall** (`base`): an army base overrun by the dead, cleared down to a few big landmarks (the hangar, headquarters with its mast and helipad, the water tower, two huts, parked tanks and the fuel tanks) with open ground between. You and 5 rivals can't hurt each other; everyone tries to outlast the horde, and the last car running wins. There are more zombies every minute (up to 480 at once, 270 on phones), and they get faster and hungrier; at 90 s they blow holes in the walls. Up to 4 cling on at once and drag a car down (swerve hard to throw them off); they splat under a fast car, are shredded by saws and blades, and drop to a couple of machine-gun rounds. Far-off zombies are drawn with a lighter model. A round lasts about 2.5 minutes. Win, and **Keep going** (or Enter) carries on the same round on your own, repaired, against a horde that keeps coming, until it gets you; your best survival time is kept and shown. Scrap for each zombie, each minute survived and your place.
- **Arena: Hammerdown Arena** (`arena`, free-for-all): a demolition derby pit, 672 by 440 voxels of mud (twice the old floor), ringed by concrete barriers, tyre walls and stacked wrecks, with grandstands, floodlights, the announcer's booth and a scoreboard hung over the middle showing the clock and the top three. You and 7 rivals, every car against every other, for 3 minutes: a wrecked car comes back after 3 s at the spawn point furthest from everyone and can't be hurt for 2.5 s; most wrecks wins (a wreck by a trap counts for whoever last hit that car), with scrap by place (400 down to 20) on top of 100 a wreck. The traps sit round the edge and each warns before it hurts and shows on the radar: a three-bladed sweeper (its eye holds the Hammerdown strike, a super weapon that marks and blasts the three nearest cars), a swinging hammer on each long side, flame jets in two corners, a crane magnet over the fuel depot, the press fed by conveyor belts, and a boneyard of loose wrecks; boost strips point into the pit, and pickups (repair, nitro, overcharge, scrap) wait beside the traps. Every 45 s the announcer calls a 15-second show event. Rules in `BRAWL` (`modes/brawl.js`); design notes and test results in `docs/arena.md`. It stands on New York's bigger world, but builds none of the city.

Hidden maps, still open by address: **Crumbletown** (`?map=town`, Monster Mayhem's whole town with waves of rivals and the garage between waves), **Crumbletown Circuit** (`?map=crumble`, a street race round Crumbletown's edge) and **Bowtie Raceway** (`?map=bowtie`, a figure-of-eight circuit with a crossover, where grass and gravel slow cars without off-road or monster tyres). The race and the arena stand on New York's 1024-square world (the page sets the engine's `__NYC__` flag as it loads, in `flags.js`; phones get the painted ground at half size); the other maps on Crumbletown's.

## The garage
A car is a body plus a part in each slot. The idea follows the big car-building brawlers, made simpler for a browser game:

- **Body** (Scrapper hatchback, Bruiser station wagon, Jackal dune buggy, Viper muscle car, Duchess fifties land yacht, Rhino boxy 4x4, Hauler pickup, Stiletto wedge sports car, Mule panel van, Mammoth school bus, Kingpin semi truck) sets health, speed and handling, and how much **Power** the car has.
- Every **main gun** on the turret (machine gun, minigun, scattergun, flamethrower), **roof** weapon (twin guns, rocket rack, arc coil, mortar), **door** weapon (a gun, a rocket tube, a shotgun or a flamethrower on each door, circular saws on arms out of the doors, or spikes on the hubs), **heavy weapon** (rocket pod, cannon, mine layer, seeker missiles), **front** (bumper, ram plow, spike ram, buzz saws) and **gadget** (nitro, repair kit, shield, oil slick, jump jets) uses some Power. A build can't use more Power than its body has, so a better body is what lets you carry more. Roof and door weapons are fixed: they only turn a little either side of straight ahead, so you point the car at what they shoot.
- **Armour** (light, steel plates, battle cage) adds health, and **wheels** (street, off-road, racing slicks, monster tyres) and **engines** (turbo four, big diesel, big V8, supercharged V8, jet turbine) change grip, speed and pull. The engine tab lifts the bonnet (or the engine cover on a mid-engined car) to show the engine fitted; the diesel puffs black smoke when floored and the turbine whines. Armour and big wheels add weight, and weight costs speed and acceleration.
- **Paint**: 14 colours, a second colour and 6 patterns (stripes, flames, camo, checkers, rust), free.

Scrap comes from wrecking cars (100), clearing waves (50 a wave), finishing places in the race and on the base, zombies, three wrecks in a row (75) and flattening buildings. A new garage starts with 400. Scrap, the parts bought and the build are kept in the browser (localStorage). Every part shows on the car: the armour is bolted on plates and window bars, the wheels and guns are their own models.

The rivals are built from the same parts, better ones each wave.

The garage is a workshop of its own: the car stands on a turntable, and the camera flies to whichever part you're changing. Drag to turn the car, or tap a glowing spot on it to open that slot. A part you pick bolts on with a drop and a clank, the stat bars slide and show what changed, and a build rating (D up to S) sums the car up. A part you don't own goes on as a look first; tap it again to buy it. Weapon cards have a Fire button that shoots the part at a target car on the firing range through the door (engines rev, nitro boosts), and Test drive takes the build out to the yard for 30 seconds against three target cars. Three saved builds sit at the top of the panel.

## Source (`derby/src`)
- `parts.js`: the garage's catalogue. Every part, what it does and costs (`PARTS`), the Power and weight rules and the stats a build adds up to (`statsOf`), the save, and the rivals' builds for each wave.
- `carshapes.js` and `carmodel.js`: the cars, built in code at cubes a third the size of the street cars' (`H = 1/6` of a town unit). Each body takes after a kind of real car, with no makes or badges: an early-80s boxy hot hatch, a mid-70s wood-sided station wagon, an open dune buggy with a roll cage, a late-60s fastback, a late-70s square-body pickup, a late-70s wedge supercar, an early-80s panel van, a conventional school bus, a boxy 4x4 with a snorkel and a spare on the tailgate, a finned fifties land yacht and a long-nose semi-truck tractor. Each shape's `bay` says where its engine bay is: `bodyPart()` carves the bay out, lines it, and makes the bonnet over it a part of its own (`hood`) that the garage swings open. `carshapes.js` holds the body shapes and the shared builder that colours each cube by what it is and which faces show (lamps and grille, chrome, shut lines and handles, glass and pillars, arches, wood trim); `carmodel.js` turns a build into parts. Wheels, turret, guns, roof and door weapons, heavy weapons and rams are parts of their own that turn, spin and get blown off.
- `vehicle.js`: a combat car: arcade driving with sliding, the weapons it carries and where their barrels are, and the crash rules (probes round the car feel for voxels, and a car going faster than `CRASH.smash` knocks a hole with the town's `blast()`).
- `garage.js`: the garage panel (the build rating, stat bars that slide with what changed, the Power meter, a tab per slot, a card per part with its picture: try it on, fire it, then buy and fit it; three saved builds), and the glowing spots on the car.
- `garageroom.js`: the workshop (turntable, tool wall, lift, scrap pile that grows with your scrap) and the yard for test drives, built past the edge of the map. `partart.js`: the cards' pictures, drawn from each part's own model on a small renderer of its own.
- `engines.js`: the engine models in the bay (inline four, turbo four, V8, supercharged V8 with its blower up through a hole in the bonnet, straight-six diesel, jet turbine), each sized to fit its car's bay.
- `specials.js`: the newer weapons and gadgets (and the diesel's black smoke): the flamethrower's jet and burning, seeker missiles that lock on and steer, mortar shells lobbed over walls, the shield bubble, oil slicks that spin cars out, and jump jets (with a stomp on whoever is landed on).
- `audio.js`: the game's own recorded sounds, placed in the street, with a few takes of the sounds heard most (see `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
- `ai.js`: the rival drivers. They follow the map's routes to their target, cut straight across when they have a clear run, shoot when their gun is on target, ram when close, drop mines on whoever is chasing, back out when stuck, go for repair kits when hurt, and sometimes go after each other or whoever hit them last.
- `tuning.js`: crashing (`CRASH`), the waves (`ROUND`) and the rivals (`AI`).
- `maps/`: one file per map. `maps/index.js` explains what a map provides (how to build it, the painted ground, where cars start, how rivals find their way, the radar drawing) and picks the map from the start card. `maps/common.js` has the shared pieces, including a grid route-finder for open maps. `maps/circuit.js` is what the street courses share (barriers, stands, the start gantry, gates, grid, paint, radar) and `maps/racetrack.js` the race tracks' maths (the centre line, the room each side, the racing line, the crossover, and `streetLoop()` for a course along the town's streets). A map marked `hidden` stays off the start card but opens with `?map=`; a map can give `radarBox` to show only part of a big world on the radar. A map can also say where the ground is rough (`surface(x, z)`, which tyres scale by their `roughK`) and list pickups and hazards.
- `modes/`: one file per set of rules (`brawl.js` is the arena's free-for-all). `modes/waves.js` explains what a mode provides (start, step, what happens when a car or the player is wrecked, the HUD and the results). `waves.js` is the town and arena's waves, `race.js` the race (with the rivals' race driver in `racer.js`), `survival.js` the last car standing on the base. A mode can also say `friendly` (cars can't hurt each other, and shots pass through them) and give `targets()` for the aim assist to pick from.
- `zombies.js`: the horde. Three looks drawn as one instanced mesh each and posed on the graphics card, a shared route field that steers them all at once, clinging, splatting and dying to guns and blasts. Tuning in `HORDE`.
- `sturdy.js`: how much the buildings take (the derby's own blast for buildings, how much a hole or a fire takes out, when a storey gives way), tuned by `BUILDINGS` in `tuning.js`.
- `drift.js`: skid marks, tyre smoke and the drift meter; the slide itself is in `vehicle.js`, tuned by `DRIFT`.
- `ground.js` and `dirt.js`: the floor, and what the wheels do to it. A map with `groundDetail: 'dirt'` (the arena's pit, the base's open ground) gets fine grain and stones up close (a tile made as the page loads), damp patches that shine, and ruts from every wheel that build up over a round (`rutBox` says where); the driven wheels throw clods, grit and dust when they spin, slide or turn hard, fast cars trail dust, and big hits and blasts throw a burst (a blast digs a crater too). With `groundDetail: { hard: true }` (the race's streets, the base's concrete, road and runway) the hard ground gets a fine grain of its own, the painted fleck softened (`deFleck`), and rubber from tyres that brake, slide or go fast, which builds up along the racing line; wheels carry mud off the dirt onto it. `wet` (the base) darkens the hard ground and gives it a sheen, and puddles throw spray. On every map a car grinding along a wall or another car throws sparks and flakes of its paint. The ground paint's alpha says what is dirt, wet, hard or standing water. All of it scales with the quality step.
- `streaks.js`: faint speed streaks round the edge of the screen when the player's car is near its top speed in the race (one draw, nothing at the lightest steps; the camera itself is left alone).
- `flags.js`: imported first, before the engine's world: picks New York's bigger world for the race and the arena (and tells the meshing worker), and the town's for every other map.
- `quality.js`: the picture quality steps (pixels, shadows, glow, far detail) and the frame-rate check that moves between them.
- `pickups.js` and `hazards.js`: pickups (repair, nitro, overcharge, scrap, the Hammerdown strike) and hazards (fire grates, the press, explosive barrels, boost strips, sweeper, hammer, flame jets, magnet, conveyor belts, and timed show events) for any map that lists them; `steerClear()` keeps rivals out of live ones.
- `../docs/arena.md`: what vehicle combat games do with their arenas, with sources, and how Hammerdown Arena uses it.
- `main.js`: the scene and light (as Monster Mayhem's), the map and its rules, the guns (`units/src/fx.js`) and what their hits do, aim assist and the phones' auto-aim, the arc coil, blades, saws, mines and gadgets, the touch controls, car against car crashes, the garage (its camera and lighting, parts bolting on, the firing range, the test drive and the target cars), the HUD, the radar and the results card.
- `page.html`: the HUD, the garage panel and the start card. `build.mjs`: bundles everything into one page.
